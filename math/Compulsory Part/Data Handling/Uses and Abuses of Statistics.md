# Uses and Abuses of Statistics

## Populations and Samples

- **Population**: the entire group of interest
- **Sample**: a subset of the population used to collect data
- Findings from a sample are generalised to the population

## Sampling Techniques

### Probability Sampling

Every member has a known, non-zero chance of being selected.

| Method | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| Simple random sampling | Each member equally likely |
| Systematic sampling | Every $k$th member selected |
| Stratified sampling | Population divided into strata, random sample from each |
| Cluster sampling | Population divided into clusters, entire clusters selected |

### Non-Probability Sampling

Selection is not random.

| Method | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| Convenience sampling | Easily accessible members chosen |
| Quota sampling | Pre-set quotas for subgroups filled non-randomly |
| Voluntary response sampling | Members self-select |

## Questionnaire Design

Factors affecting **validity** and **reliability**:

- **Type** of questions (open-ended vs closed)
- **Wording** (leading questions, jargon, ambiguity)
- **Ordering** of questions (priming effects)
- **Response options** (balanced scales, exhaustive categories)

## Uses of Statistics

- Summarising and describing data
- Drawing inferences and making predictions
- Testing hypotheses in scientific research
- Supporting decision-making in business, government, and daily life

## Abuses of Statistics

- **Biased sampling** — conclusions do not represent the population
- **Misleading graphs** — distorted scales, truncated axes, inappropriate chart types
- **Cherry-picking data** — selective reporting of favourable results
- **Correlation mistaken for causation**
- **Overgeneralisation** — applying results beyond the sampled population
- **Leading questions** in surveys

## Assessing Statistical Investigations

When evaluating claims in news media or research reports, consider:
- Who conducted the study and what was their purpose?
- Was the sample representative and sufficiently large?
- Were the questions fair and unbiased?
- Are the conclusions supported by the data?
- Are graphs and statistics presented accurately?
