# Personal Financial Management

## 1. Time Value of Money

> [!note] Core Concept
> A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow because of its **earning potential** (interest/investment returns).

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Present Value (PV)** | The current value of a future sum of money |
| **Future Value (FV)** | The value of a current sum of money at a specified date in the future |
| **Compounding** | Calculating future value by applying interest over multiple periods |
| **Discounting** | Calculating present value by removing interest from future cash flows |

### Formulas

**Future Value:**
$$FV = PV \times (1 + r)^n$$

**Present Value:**
$$PV = \frac{FV}{(1 + r)^n}$$

**Net Present Value (NPV):**
$$NPV = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} - \text{Initial Investment}$$

Where: $r$ = discount rate, $n$ = number of periods, $CF_t$ = cash flow at time $t$

> [!example] Worked Example
> You expect to receive \$10,000 in 3 years. The discount rate is 8%.
>
> $PV = \frac{10{,}000}{(1.08)^3} = \frac{10{,}000}{1.2597} = \$7{,}938.32$
>
> The present value is **\$7,938.32** — this is what you should be willing to pay today for that future sum.

### Nominal vs Effective Rate of Return

| Type | Definition | Formula |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Nominal rate** | Stated annual interest rate (before compounding) | — |
| **Effective rate** | Actual annual rate after compounding | $r_{eff} = (1 + \frac{r_{nom}}{m})^m - 1$ |

Where $m$ = number of compounding periods per year.

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> When comparing investment options, always use the **effective rate** for a fair comparison, as different products may compound at different frequencies.

## 2. Consumer Credit

### Types of Consumer Credit

| Type | Description | Pros | Cons |
|------|-------------|------|------|
| **Credit cards** | Revolving credit facility | Convenience, interest-free period | High interest if not paid in full |
| **Personal loans** | Fixed amount repaid in instalments | Predictable repayments | Interest charges, commitment |
| **Hire purchase** | Buy goods and pay in instalments | Spread cost over time | Total cost is higher |
| **Mortgage** | Loan for property purchase | Enables home ownership | Long-term commitment, risk of repossession |
| **Overdraft** | Borrow beyond bank balance | Flexibility | High interest, fees |

### Importance of Good Credit Record

> [!warning] Key Point
> A good personal credit record is essential for:
> - Obtaining loans at favourable interest rates
> - Renting property
> - Job applications (some employers check credit history)
> - Future financial flexibility

Maintain a good credit record by:
- Paying bills on time
- Not exceeding credit limits
- Avoiding frequent credit applications
- Regularly checking credit reports

## 3. Personal Financial Planning and Investment

### Why Financial Planning Matters

- Achieve financial goals (education, housing, retirement)
- Build wealth over time through compounding
- Protect against unexpected events (insurance)
- Ensure financial security at different life stages

### Investment Options

| Investment | Risk | Return | Liquidity |
|------------|------|--------|-----------|
| **Savings/Term deposits** | Very low | Low | High |
| **Bonds/Debentures** | Low–Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Stocks (shares)** | High | Potentially high | High (listed) |

> [!important] Risk-Return Tradeoff
> Higher potential returns come with higher risk. There is no investment that offers high returns with zero risk.

### Mandatory Provident Fund (MPF)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Retirement protection for employees |
| **Contributions** | 5% from employee + 5% from employer (on relevant income) |
| **Mandatory** | All employees and self-employed persons must contribute |
| **Vesting** | Employer contributions subject to vesting period |
| **Withdrawal** | At age 65, or early withdrawal on specific grounds |

## 4. Stock Trading as an Investment

### Factors Affecting Share Prices

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Company performance** | Better profits → higher share price |
| **Interest rates** | Higher rates → lower share prices (opportunity cost) |
| **Economic conditions** | Strong economy → higher prices |
| **Industry trends** | Sector growth → higher prices |
| **Investor sentiment** | Optimism → higher prices |
| **Political events** | Stability → higher prices |

### Stock Trading Platforms in Hong Kong

| Platform | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Main Board** | For established, profitable companies |
| **GEM (Growth Enterprise Market)** | For smaller, high-growth companies with shorter track record |

### Hang Seng Index (HSI)

> [!note] Definition
> The **Hang Seng Index** is the benchmark stock market index tracking the performance of the largest companies listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. It serves as a barometer of the HK stock market and economy.
