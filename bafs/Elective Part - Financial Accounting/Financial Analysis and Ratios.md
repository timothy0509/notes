# Financial Analysis and Ratios

> [!abstract] Overview
> Financial analysis uses accounting ratios to evaluate a company's performance across profitability, liquidity, solvency, management efficiency, and return on investment. This topic covers all major ratios, their interpretation, and the functions and limitations of ratio analysis.

---

## 1. Functions of Financial Analysis

1. **Express meaningful relationships** — ratios show the relationship between financial items
2. **Calculate relative magnitude** — compare performance over time or between companies
3. **Facilitate comparison** — enable benchmarking against industry standards
4. **Aid decision-making** — help investors, creditors, and management make informed choices

---

## 2. Profitability Ratios

> [!tip] Interpretation
> Higher is generally better, but consider industry norms and economic conditions.

| Ratio | Formula | What It Shows |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Mark-up** | $\frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{COGS}} \times 100\%$ | Profit margin above cost |
| **Gross profit ratio** | $\frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100\%$ | Percentage of sales remaining after COGS |
| **Net profit ratio** | $\frac{\text{Net profit before tax}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100\%$ | Overall profitability from sales |
| **Return on capital employed (ROCE)** | $\frac{\text{PBIT}}{\text{Average capital employed}} \times 100\%$ | Return generated on all capital invested |

> [!note] Capital Employed
> - **Sole proprietorship:** Capital balance
> - **Partnership:** Capital accounts + current accounts
> - **Limited company:** Non-current liabilities + Shareholders' equity

---

## 3. Liquidity Ratios

> [!tip] Interpretation
> Higher is generally better, indicating ability to meet short-term obligations.

| Ratio | Formula | What It Shows |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Current ratio** | $\frac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$ | Ability to pay short-term debts (target: 2:1) |
| **Quick ratio (Acid test)** | $\frac{\text{Current assets} - \text{Inventory}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$ | Ability to pay without selling inventory (target: 1:1) |

---

## 4. Management Efficiency Ratios

> [!tip] Interpretation
> Higher turnover = better efficiency. Lower collection/repayment periods = better management.

| Ratio | Formula | What It Shows |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Inventory turnover** | $\frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average inventory}}$ | How quickly inventory is sold |
| **Avg trade receivables collection period** | $\frac{\text{Average trade receivables}}{\text{Credit sales}} \times 365$ | Days to collect from customers |
| **Trade receivables turnover** | $\frac{\text{Credit sales}}{\text{Average trade receivables}}$ | Times receivables are collected per year |
| **Avg trade payables repayment period** | $\frac{\text{Average trade payables}}{\text{Credit purchases}} \times 365$ | Days taken to pay suppliers |
| **Trade payables turnover** | $\frac{\text{Credit purchases}}{\text{Average trade payables}}$ | Times payables are paid per year |
| **Total assets turnover** | $\frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Total assets}}$ | Efficiency in using assets to generate sales |

> [!info] Taking Averages
> For ratios involving balance sheet items, use the **average of opening and closing balances** to account for changes during the year.

---

## 5. Solvency Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | What It Shows |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Gearing ratio** | $\frac{\text{Non-current liabilities} + \text{Preference shares}}{\text{Non-current liabilities} + \text{Shareholders' equity}} \times 100\%$ | Proportion of debt financing (lower = less risky) |

> [!warning] High Gearing
> A gearing ratio above 50% indicates high financial risk — the company relies more on debt than equity. This increases the risk of insolvency during economic downturns.

---

## 6. Return on Investment Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | What It Shows |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Earnings per share (EPS)** | $\frac{\text{Net profit after tax} - \text{Preference dividend}}{\text{Number of ordinary shares}}$ | Profit attributable to each ordinary share |
| **Dividend cover** | $\frac{\text{Net profit after tax} - \text{Preference dividend}}{\text{Ordinary dividend paid}}$ | Times dividend could be paid from current earnings |
| **Price-earnings ratio (P/E)** | $\frac{\text{Market price per share}}{\text{EPS}}$ | How much investors are willing to pay per \$1 of earnings |

---

## 7. Functions and Limitations

### Functions

- Facilitate **comparison** between periods and companies
- Identify **trends** in performance
- Aid **decision-making** for investors and creditors
- Highlight **strengths and weaknesses**

### Limitations

| Limitation | Explanation |
|------------|-------------|
| **Cannot tell the whole story** | Ratios are summaries; they don't capture qualitative factors |
| **Not comparable across industries** | Different industries have different norms |
| **Based on historical data** | Past performance doesn't guarantee future results |
| **No universal norms** | "Good" ratios vary by industry and economic conditions |
| **Cannot identify specific problems** | Ratios highlight symptoms, not causes |
| **Affected by accounting policies** | Different depreciation methods or inventory valuation affect ratios |

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> When asked to evaluate a company's performance using ratios, always:
> 1. **Calculate** the ratios (if not given)
> 2. **Compare** with previous year or industry average
> 3. **Comment** on what the ratios indicate
> 4. **Explain** possible reasons for the trends
> 5. **Recommend** actions based on findings
