# Incomplete Records

> [!abstract] Overview
> Some businesses, particularly sole proprietorships and SMEs, do not maintain full double entry records. This topic covers how to determine profit, calculate missing values, and prepare financial statements from incomplete information.

---

## 1. Determining Profit from Statement of Affairs

> [!note] Definition
> A **statement of affairs** is a statement listing all assets and liabilities at a particular date, used to calculate the owner's equity when full records are not maintained.

### Formula

$$\text{Profit/Loss} = \text{Closing capital} - \text{Opening capital} + \text{Drawings} - \text{Additional capital introduced}$$

| Component | Source |
|-----------|--------|
| Opening capital | Statement of affairs at start of period |
| Closing capital | Statement of affairs at end of period |
| Drawings | Records or bank statements |
| Additional capital | Records or bank statements |

> [!example] Worked Example
> | | Start of Year | End of Year |
> |---|---|---|
> | Total assets | \$80,000 | \$120,000 |
> | Total liabilities | \$30,000 | \$35,000 |
> | **Capital** | **\$50,000** | **\$85,000** |
>
> Drawings during year = \$20,000
> Additional capital introduced = \$5,000
>
> Profit = \$85,000 − \$50,000 + \$20,000 − \$5,000 = **\$50,000**

---

## 2. Calculating Missing Values

### Sales from Total Receivables

$$\text{Sales} = \text{Closing receivables} + \text{Cash received from customers} - \text{Opening receivables}$$

### Purchases from Total Payables

$$\text{Purchases} = \text{Closing payables} + \text{Cash paid to suppliers} - \text{Opening payables}$$

### Cash Balance

$$\text{Closing cash} = \text{Opening cash} + \text{Cash received} - \text{Cash paid}$$

### Inventory

When closing inventory is not given, it can sometimes be calculated from:
- Mark-up on sales
- Gross profit percentage
- Physical count

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> In incomplete records questions, start by listing all known values and then work backwards using the formulas above. Build a **total account** (e.g. total receivables account) to track movements.

---

## 3. Preparing Financial Statements

### Steps

1. Calculate missing sales/purchases using total accounts
2. Prepare an income statement using available information
3. Prepare a statement of affairs for the balance sheet
4. Use the profit figure to balance the capital account

### Format for Incomplete Records Income Statement

| | \$ |
|---|---|
| **Sales** (calculated) | X |
| Less: Cost of goods sold | |
| Opening inventory | X |
| Add: Purchases (calculated) | X |
| Less: Closing inventory | (X) |
| **Gross profit** | X |
| Less: Expenses | |
| (List known expenses) | (X) |
| **Net profit** | X |

> [!warning] Common Pitfall
> Do not assume that all cash received is from sales, or all cash paid is for purchases. Some may be for capital expenditure, drawings, or loan repayments. Always分析 the nature of each cash flow.
