# Accounting Principles and Conventions

> [!abstract] Overview
> Accounting principles and conventions provide the framework for preparing financial statements. They ensure consistency, comparability, and reliability of financial information across different businesses and periods.

---

## 1. Key Accounting Principles

### Business Entity

> [!note] Definition
> The business is treated as a **separate entity** from its owner(s). Personal transactions of the owner are kept separate from business transactions.

**Implication:** Owner's personal assets, liabilities, and drawings are not included in the business's financial statements.

### Going Concern

> [!note] Definition
> The business is assumed to continue operating **indefinitely** into the foreseeable future. There is no intention to liquidate or significantly reduce operations.

**Implication:** Assets are valued at cost (not liquidation value), and liabilities are classified as current/non-current based on expected timing.

### Historical Cost

> [!note] Definition
> Assets are recorded at their **original cost** (the amount paid to acquire them), not at current market value.

**Implication:** Even if an asset's market value increases, it remains on the books at cost (less depreciation).

### Consistency

> [!note] Definition
> Once an accounting policy or method is adopted, it should be applied **consistently** from one period to the next.

**Implication:** If depreciation method is straight-line in Year 1, it should remain straight-line in Year 2 unless there is a valid reason to change (and the change is disclosed).

### Accrual

> [!note] Definition
> Revenue and expenses are recorded when they are **earned or incurred**, not when cash is received or paid.

**Implication:** Accrued income, accrued expenses, prepaid expenses, and unearned revenue must be recognised.

### Matching

> [!note] Definition
> Expenses should be **matched** with the revenues they help to generate in the same period.

**Implication:** Cost of goods sold is matched against sales revenue; depreciation of production equipment is matched against output.

### Realisation

> [!note] Definition
> Revenue is recognised when it is **realised** — typically when goods are delivered or services are rendered, and the amount can be reliably measured.

**Implication:** Unrealised gains (e.g. increase in market value of property) are not recorded as revenue.

### Prudence (Conservatism)

> [!note] Definition
> When in doubt, choose the method that is **least likely to overstate** assets or income.

**Implication:** Provide for probable losses, but do not anticipate gains. Value inventory at lower of cost and NRV.

### Materiality

> [!note] Definition
> An item is **material** if its omission or misstatement could influence the economic decisions of users.

**Implication:** Small items (e.g. stationery) can be expensed immediately rather than capitalised and depreciated.

### Money Measurement

> [!note] Definition
> Only transactions that can be expressed in **monetary terms** are recorded in the financial statements.

**Implication:** Non-financial factors (employee morale, brand reputation) are not recorded, even though they may be very important.

## 2. Summary Table

| Principle/Convention | Core Idea | Why It Matters |
|----------------------|-----------|----------------|
| Business entity | Business ≠ owner | Prevents mixing personal and business affairs |
| Going concern | Business will continue | Justifies cost-based valuation |
| Historical cost | Record at original cost | Provides verifiable, objective values |
| Consistency | Same methods each period | Enables comparison across periods |
| Accrual | Record when earned/incurred | More accurate profit measurement |
| Matching | Match expenses to revenues | Proper profit determination |
| Realisation | Record revenue when earned | Prevents premature revenue recognition |
| Prudence | Don't overstate assets/income | Protects users from misleading info |
| Materiality | Focus on significant items | Avoids unnecessary complexity |
| Money measurement | Only record monetary items | Defines the scope of accounting |

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> DSE questions often ask you to **apply** these principles to specific scenarios. For example: "Explain which accounting principle is being applied when inventory is valued at the lower of cost and NRV." (Answer: Prudence)

---

## 3. Shortcomings of Accounting Principles

| Principle | Limitation |
|-----------|------------|
| Historical cost | Does not reflect current value (inflation) |
| Prudence | May understate assets/income |
| Consistency | May prevent adoption of better methods |
| Money measurement | Ignores non-financial factors |
| Going concern | May not be valid if business is failing |
