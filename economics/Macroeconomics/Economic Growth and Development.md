# Economic Growth and Development

## Measurement of Economic Growth

### Growth in Real GDP

$$\text{Economic growth rate} = \frac{\text{Real GDP}_t - \text{Real GDP}_{t-1}}{\text{Real GDP}_{t-1}} \times 100\%$$

- **Real GDP** measures total output adjusted for inflation
- Growth measures the expansion of the economy's **productive capacity**

### Per Capita Real GDP

$$\text{Per capita real GDP} = \frac{\text{Real GDP}}{\text{Population}}$$

> [!tip] Per capita real GDP better reflects **changes in average living standards** than total GDP, as it accounts for population changes.

### Human Development Index (HDI)

The **HDI** is a composite measure capturing three dimensions of development:

| Dimension | Indicator |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Health** | Life expectancy at birth |
| **Education** | Mean years of schooling + expected years of schooling |
| **Living standards** | GNI per capita (PPP, log-transformed) |

$$\text{HDI} = \frac{1}{3}(\text{Health index} + \text{Education index} + \text{Income index})$$

> [!info] HDI ranges from 0 to 1. A higher HDI indicates greater human development. Unlike GDP per capita alone, HDI captures **non-monetary** aspects of well-being.

### GDP vs HDI Comparison

| Measure | What It Captures | Limitation |
|---------|-----------------|------------|
| **GDP per capita** | Market value of output per person | Ignores inequality, health, education, environment |
| **HDI** | Health, education, income | Still ignores inequality, environment, political freedom |

## Factors Affecting Growth

### Input Factors (Sources of Growth)

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Physical capital** | Machinery, factories, infrastructure — investment in capital raises labour productivity |
| **Human capital** | Education, training, health — improves labour quality |
| **Natural resources** | Land, oil, minerals — can provide resource base for growth |
| **Technological change** | Innovation, R&D, knowledge — the **key driver** of sustained growth |

### The Production Function Framework

$$Y = A \cdot F(K, L, H, N)$$

- $Y$ = output
- $A$ = total factor productivity (technology)
- $K$ = physical capital
- $L$ = labour
- $H$ = human capital
- $N$ = natural resources

> [!important] In the long run, **technological progress** ($A$) is the most important source of sustained growth — diminishing returns to $K$ alone cannot sustain growth indefinitely.

### Policies to Promote Growth

| Policy | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Encourage saving and investment** | Higher saving rate $\to$ more capital accumulation |
| **Attract foreign direct investment (FDI)** | Brings capital, technology, and management know-how |
| **Promote international trade** | Expands markets, transfers technology |
| **Invest in education and training** | Builds human capital |
| **Control population growth** | Prevents dilution of capital per worker |
| **Establish property rights** | Incentivises investment and innovation |
| **Support R&D** | Drives technological change |
| **Sound macroeconomic policies** | Stable prices, sustainable fiscal position |

## Desirability and Costs of Growth

### Benefits of Economic Growth

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Higher living standards** | More goods and services per person |
| **Reduced poverty** | Growth creates employment and income |
| **Improved public services** | More tax revenue for education, health, infrastructure |
| **Technological advancement** | Innovation improves quality of life |
| **Greater economic stability** | More resources to buffer against shocks |

### Costs and Trade-Offs

> [!warning] Growth is not free. Important trade-offs must be considered.

| Cost | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| **Sacrifice of current consumption** | To invest, society must save (consume less now) |
| **Income inequality** | Growth may disproportionately benefit the wealthy |
| **Resource exhaustion** | Non-renewable resources are depleted |
| **Environmental degradation** | Pollution, climate change, habitat loss |
| **Social disruption** | Urbanisation, cultural change, job displacement |

### The Growth vs Development Debate

> [!example] **GDP per capita may rise while HDI stagnates** if growth does not translate into better health or education outcomes. This is the distinction between **growth** (more output) and **development** (broader improvement in well-being).

### Sustainable Development

**Sustainable development**: Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Key considerations:
- **Green GDP**: Adjusting GDP for environmental degradation
- **Natural capital accounting**: Valuing natural resources
- **Renewable energy transition**: Decoupling growth from fossil fuels
- **Intergenerational equity**: Fairness across generations

## International and Regional Comparison

### Developed vs Developing Countries

| Indicator | Developed | Developing |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| High HDI (0.8+) | Moderate to low HDI |
| Diversified economy | Often reliant on primary commodities |
| Strong institutions | Weaker institutional frameworks |
| Higher capital per worker | Lower capital per worker |
| Service-dominated | Agriculture/industry-dominated |

### East Asian Growth Experience

- **Four Asian Tigers** (Hong Kong, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan): Rapid growth 1960s–1990s
- High saving and investment rates
- Export-oriented industrialisation
- Heavy investment in education
- Strong institutional frameworks

> [!info] Hong Kong's growth model: free trade, rule of law, low taxes, open capital markets, and a service-based economy. Growth has slowed as the economy matured (convergence effect).
