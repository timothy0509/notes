# International Trade and Finance

## Free Trade and Comparative Advantage

### Absolute Advantage vs Comparative Advantage

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Absolute advantage** | A country can produce a good using **fewer resources** than another |
| **Comparative advantage** | A country can produce a good at a **lower opportunity cost** than another |

> [!important] The **principle of comparative advantage** (David Ricardo): Even if one country has an absolute advantage in ALL goods, both countries can still gain from trade by specialising in what they do **relatively best**.

### Conditions for Mutually Beneficial Trade

1. Opportunity cost ratios differ between countries
2. The **terms of trade** (international price ratio) lies between the two countries' domestic opportunity cost ratios
3. Each country specialises in its comparative advantage good

### Gains from Trade

- **Consumption gains**: Countries can consume beyond their PPF
- **Production gains**: More efficient allocation of resources
- **Greater variety** of goods and services
- **Technology transfer** and knowledge spillovers

> [!example] If Country A produces 1 food or 2 cloth, and Country B produces 1 food or 1 cloth:
> - Country A has comparative advantage in cloth (OC = 0.5 food)
> - Country B has comparative advantage in food (OC = 1 cloth)
> - If terms of trade = 1 food for 1.5 cloth, both gain

## Hong Kong's Trade Pattern

- **Entrepôt trade** historically significant; now re-export dominance
- Mainland China is Hong Kong's **largest trading partner**
- Hong Kong is a **free port** (no tariffs on most goods)
- Trade in services (financial, logistics, tourism) increasingly important
- Trade-to-GDP ratio exceeds 300%, highlighting HK's extreme openness

> [!note] Hong Kong's **re-export** trade means most goods passing through HK are never consumed domestically. This distorts trade statistics.

## Trade Barriers

### Types of Trade Barriers

| Barrier | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Tariff** | Tax on imported goods |
| **Quota** | Physical limit on quantity of imports |
| **Import surcharge** | Additional percentage charge on imports |
| **Embargo** | Complete ban on trade with a country |
| **Non-tariff barriers** | Standards, licensing, red tape |

### Effects of a Tariff (Small Open Economy)

| Effect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Price** | Domestic price rises to world price + tariff |
| **Domestic production** | Rises (protected domestic firms produce more) |
| **Domestic consumption** | Falls (higher prices reduce demand) |
| **Imports** | Decrease |
| **Government revenue** | Tariff revenue earned |
| **Welfare** | **Net welfare loss** (deadweight loss from overproduction + underconsumption) |

> [!warning] Tariffs create **deadweight losses** and reduce overall economic welfare. They benefit domestic producers but harm consumers.

### Trade Barriers Faced by Hong Kong

- Discriminatory tariffs and non-tariff barriers from trading partners
- Antidumping investigations against HK exports
- Restrictions on HK-origin goods post-handover in some markets

### HK's Attempts to Overcome Trade Barriers

- **Trade promotion** through Hong Kong Trade Development Council (HKTDC)
- **Bilateral and multilateral negotiations** by HKSAR government
- Active role in **WTO** as a separate member
- **Free Trade Agreements** (with New Zealand, EFTA, ASEAN, etc.)
- **Investment promotion** and protection agreements

## Balance of Payments (BOP)

### Structure of BOP

| Account | Components |
|---------|-----------|
| **Current Account** | Goods, Services, Income, Current Transfers |
| **Capital and Financial Account** | Direct investment, Portfolio investment, Financial derivatives, Other investment, Reserve assets |

### Key Identities

$$\text{Current account} + \text{Capital and financial account} + \text{Net errors and omissions} = 0$$

$$\text{Trade balance} = \text{Exports of goods} - \text{Imports of goods}$$

$$\text{Current account balance} = \text{Trade balance} + \text{Services balance} + \text{Income balance} + \text{Current transfers balance}$$

> [!important] The BOP **always balances** in an accounting sense. A current account deficit is exactly offset by a capital and financial account surplus (and vice versa), except for statistical discrepancies.

### Trade Surplus vs Deficit

- **Trade surplus**: Exports > Imports (positive contribution to GDP)
- **Trade deficit**: Imports > Exports (subtracts from GDP)

## Exchange Rates

### Meaning

The **exchange rate** is the price of one currency in terms of another.

### Appreciation vs Depreciation

| Movement | Definition |
|----------|-----------|
| **Appreciation** | Currency value rises under **floating** exchange rate |
| **Depreciation** | Currency value falls under floating exchange rate |
| **Revaluation** | Currency value rises under **fixed** exchange rate |
| **Devaluation** | Currency value falls under fixed exchange rate |

### Effects on Import/Export Prices and Volumes

| Scenario | Export Prices | Export Volume | Import Prices | Import Volume |
|----------|--------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| **HK\$ appreciates** | Rise (less competitive) | Fall | Fall (cheaper) | Rise |
| **HK\$ depreciates** | Fall (more competitive) | Rise | Rise (dearer) | Fall |

> [!tip] The net effect on the trade balance depends on the **Marshall-Lerner condition**: if the sum of export and import demand elasticities exceeds 1, depreciation improves the trade balance.

## Linked Exchange Rate System in Hong Kong

### Brief History

- 1972–1974: HK\$ pegged to US\$ (floated briefly in 1974)
- 1983: **Linked exchange rate system** established at 7.80 HKD/USD after confidence crisis
- Current trading band: 7.75–7.85 HKD/USD

### Currency Board System

> [!info] A **Currency Board** requires full backing of the monetary base with foreign reserves. Every Hong Kong dollar issued must be backed by an equivalent amount of US dollars.

Key features:
1. The Exchange Fund determines the **convertibility rate**
2. The HKMA guarantees conversion at the **convertibility zone** (7.75–7.85)
3. Disciplines monetary policy: HK interest rates follow US rates
4. Promotes **credibility and stability** of the currency

### Note-Issuing Mechanism

Three **note-issuing banks** (HSBC, Bank of China, Standard Chartered) issue Hong Kong dollar notes under the supervision of the HKMA.

Commercial banks pay US dollars to the Exchange Fund at the linked rate to receive Certificates of Indebtedness, which authorise them to issue HKD notes.
