# Measurement of Economic Performance

## National Income

**National income** measures the total value of goods and services produced by an economy over a period of time (usually one year).

### GDP vs GNI

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **GDP** (Gross Domestic Product) | Total value of final goods & services produced **within a territory**, regardless of who owns the factors of production |
| **GNI** (Gross National Income) | GDP **plus net income from abroad** (income earned by residents from overseas minus income earned by non-residents domestically) |

$$GNI = GDP + \text{Net income from abroad}$$

> [!info] For Hong Kong, GDP and GNI differ because of significant outward and inward investment flows. GNI is typically slightly lower than GDP.

## Three Approaches to Measuring GDP

### 1. Expenditure Approach

$$Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$$

| Component | Meaning | Examples |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| $C$ | Private Consumption Expenditure | Food, rent, transport |
| $I$ | Gross Domestic Fixed Capital Formation + change in inventories | Machinery, buildings, unsold goods |
| $G$ | Government Consumption Expenditure | Salaries of civil servants, public services |
| $X - M$ | Net Exports of Goods and Services | Exports minus imports |

> [!warning] Government **transfer payments** (e.g., CSSA) are not part of $G$ because they are not payments for goods/services produced.

### 2. Production (Value-Added) Approach

Sum of **value-added** of all resident producing units.

$$\text{Value-added} = \text{Output} - \text{Intermediate Inputs}$$

> [!example] A baker buys flour for \$20 and sells bread for \$50. Value-added = \$30. This avoids double-counting.

### 3. Income Approach

Sum of **factor incomes** earned by residents:

$$GDP = \text{Wages} + \text{Rent} + \text{Interest} + \text{Profit} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Indirect taxes} - \text{Subsidies}$$

> [!note] The three approaches should yield the **same** GDP figure, though statistical discrepancies may occur.

## Nominal vs Real GDP

| Measure | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Nominal GDP** | GDP valued at **current** market prices |
| **Real GDP** | GDP valued at **constant base-year** prices (adjusted for inflation) |

$$\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Real GDP growth rate} = \frac{\text{Real GDP}_t - \text{Real GDP}_{t-1}}{\text{Real GDP}_{t-1}} \times 100\%$$

> [!tip] Use **real GDP** (not nominal) to compare economic growth across time, because it removes the effect of price changes.

## GDP at Factor Cost vs Market Prices

$$\text{GDP at market prices} = \text{GDP at factor cost} + \text{Indirect taxes} - \text{Subsidies}$$

- **Factor cost**: value at the cost of factors of production
- **Market prices**: what consumers actually pay (includes taxes, excludes subsidies)

## Per Capita GDP

$$\text{Per capita GDP} = \frac{\text{GDP}}{\text{Population}}$$

Used for rough comparisons of **living standards** across countries, though it ignores income distribution.

## Uses and Limitations of National Income Statistics

### Uses
- Indicator of **economic growth** and **business cycles**
- Basis for **fiscal and monetary policy** formulation
- **International comparisons** of output and living standards
- Measure of **structural change** (e.g., share of services in GDP)

### Limitations

> [!warning] GDP is **not** a perfect measure of economic welfare:
> - **Non-market activities** excluded (household work, volunteer labour)
> - **Underground economy** not captured (illegal activities, tax evasion)
> - **Externalities** ignored (pollution, resource depletion)
> - **Income distribution** not reflected
> - **Quality improvements** hard to measure
> - **Leisure** not valued

## General Price Level

### CPI (Consumer Price Index)

$$\text{CPI} = \frac{\text{Cost of basket in current year}}{\text{Cost of basket in base year}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Inflation rate} = \frac{\text{CPI}_t - \text{CPI}_{t-1}}{\text{CPI}_{t-1}} \times 100\%$$

### GDP Deflator

$$\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100$$

### CPI vs GDP Deflator

| Feature | CPI | GDP Deflator |
|---------|-----|-------------|
| Basket | Fixed basket of consumer goods | All domestically produced goods |
| Imports | Included | Excluded |
| Base year updates | Periodic rebasing | Automatically updated |
| Who it affects | Households | Whole economy |

## Unemployment and Underemployment

- **Unemployment rate** = $\frac{\text{Unemployed}}{\text{Labour Force}} \times 100\%$
- **Underemployment rate** = $\frac{\text{Underemployed}}{\text{Labour Force}} \times 100\%$

> [!info] **Underemployed** workers are those working fewer than 35 hours/week but willing to work more.

## Recent Trends in Hong Kong

Hong Kong's economy has undergone significant structural change:
- Shift from manufacturing to **services** (over 90% of GDP)
- Close integration with the **Mainland China** economy
- Low unemployment historically (around 3%), rising during recessions
- Mild inflation environment, occasional deflationary pressures
