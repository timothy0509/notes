# Macroeconomic Problems and Policies

## Business Cycles

The economy fluctuates around its long-run growth trend in **business cycles**:

| Phase | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Expansion** | Rising output, employment, income; increasing consumer and business confidence |
| **Peak** | Output at maximum; inflationary pressures build; full employment |
| **Contraction** | Falling output, rising unemployment, declining investment |
| **Trough** | Lowest point of output; high unemployment; may lead to recovery |

> [!important] Business cycles are **irregular** in length and severity. Modern economies still experience short-run fluctuations despite long-run growth.

## Inflation and Deflation

### Definitions

- **Inflation**: A sustained rise in the **general price level**
- **Deflation**: A sustained fall in the general price level
- **Disinflation**: A **decrease in the rate** of inflation (prices still rising, but more slowly)

### Nominal vs Real Interest Rates

$$r = i - \pi$$

where:
- $r$ = real interest rate
- $i$ = nominal interest rate
- $\pi$ = inflation rate

> [!warning] If $\pi$ is unanticipated, lenders lose and borrowers gain when $\pi >$ expected. Fixed-income recipients are especially hurt by unanticipated inflation.

### Redistributive Effects of Inflation

| Group | Effect of Unanticipated Inflation |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| **Debtors** | Gain (repay loans with less valuable money) |
| **Creditors** | Lose (repaid with less valuable money) |
| **Workers with fixed wages** | Lose (real income falls) |
| **Owners of real assets** | May gain (asset prices rise with inflation) |
| **Savers (monetary assets)** | Lose (purchasing power of savings erodes) |

### Quantity Theory of Money

$$MV = PY$$

where:
- $M$ = money supply
- $V$ = velocity of circulation (assumed constant in the short run)
- $P$ = price level
- $Y$ = real output

Assuming $V$ and $Y$ are constant in the short run:

$$\Delta\% M \approx \Delta\% P \quad \text{(inflation rate)}$$

> [!info] The Quantity Theory implies that **inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon** (Milton Friedman). A sustained rise in $M$ without a corresponding rise in $Y$ leads to inflation.

## Unemployment

### Meaning and Measurement

$$\text{Unemployment rate} = \frac{\text{Number of unemployed}}{\text{Labour force}} \times 100\%$$

**Underemployment**: Persons working fewer than 35 hours per week who are willing and available to work more.

### Costs of Unemployment

| Category | Costs |
|----------|-------|
| **Individual** | Loss of income, loss of skills, psychological harm, health deterioration |
| **Society** | Loss of potential output (GDP gap), increased welfare spending, lost tax revenue, social unrest |

### Types of Gaps

| Gap | Condition | Characteristic |
|-----|-----------|---------------|
| **Deflationary (recessionary) gap** | $Y_e < Y_f$ | Actual output below potential; unemployment above natural rate |
| **Inflationary gap** | $Y_e > Y_f$ | Actual output above potential; economy overheating |

> [!note] The **natural rate of unemployment** (also called NAIRU) is the rate consistent with $Y = Y_f$. It includes frictional and structural unemployment.

## Fiscal Policy

### Budget Types

| Budget | $T - G$ | Effect on AD |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Balanced budget** | $T = G$ | Neutral (may be slightly expansionary) |
| **Budget surplus** | $T > G$ | Contractionary (withdraws demand) |
| **Budget deficit** | $T < G$ | Expansionary (injects demand) |

### Adam Smith's Four Principles of Taxation (Canons of Taxation)

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Economy** | Cost of collection should be low relative to revenue |
| **Equity** | Tax burden should be fair (ability to pay / benefit principle) |
| **Certainty** | Taxpayer should know when, how much, and how to pay |
| **Convenience** | Tax should be levied at a time and manner convenient to the taxpayer |

### Classification of Taxes

**By structure:**

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Direct tax** | Burden cannot be shifted | Salaries tax, profits tax |
| **Indirect tax** | Burden can be shifted (to consumers) | Salaries tax, goods and services tax |

**By rate structure:**

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Progressive** | Average tax rate rises with income | Salaries tax (marginal brackets) |
| **Proportional** | Average tax rate constant | Flat tax |
| **Regressive** | Average tax rate falls with income | GST/VAT (poor spend higher % of income) |

### Public Expenditure

Classified into:
- **Recurrent expenditure** (daily operating costs: salaries, welfare)
- **Capital expenditure** (infrastructure, buildings)

> [!tip] Fiscal policy can be **automatic** (automatic stabilisers like progressive income tax and welfare benefits) or **discretionary** (deliberate changes in $G$ or $T$).

### Effects of Fiscal Policy (AD-AS Framework)

| Policy | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Expansionary** ($G \uparrow$ or $T \downarrow$) | AD $\rightarrow$ $\to$ $Y \uparrow$, $P \uparrow$ (short run) |
| **Contractionary** ($G \downarrow$ or $T \uparrow$) | AD $\leftarrow$ $\to$ $Y \downarrow$, $P \downarrow$ (short run) |

> [!warning] **Crowding out**: Expansionary fiscal policy may raise interest rates, reducing private investment and offsetting some of the stimulus.

## Monetary Policy

### Meaning

Monetary policy involves the **central bank / monetary authority** controlling the **money supply** and **interest rates** to achieve macroeconomic objectives.

### Tools of Monetary Policy

| Tool | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Open market operations** | Buying/selling government securities to adjust monetary base |
| **Discount rate** | Interest rate charged to banks for borrowing from central bank |
| **Reserve requirements** | Changing the required reserve ratio |
| **Selective credit controls** | Margin requirements, consumer credit controls |

### Effects on Output and Price (AD-AS Model)

| Policy | Mechanism | Effect |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Expansionary** ($M_s \uparrow$) | $i \downarrow \to I$ and $C \uparrow \to AD \rightarrow \to Y \uparrow$, $P \uparrow$ |
| **Contractionary** ($M_s \downarrow$) | $i \uparrow \to I$ and $C \downarrow \to AD \leftarrow \to Y \downarrow$, $P \downarrow$ |

### Monetary Policy in Hong Kong

> [!important] Hong Kong operates a **Currency Board System** via the linked exchange rate. The HKMA does **not** have independent monetary policy — changes in HK interest rates essentially **follow US rates** to maintain the exchange rate peg.

## Fiscal vs Monetary Policy Comparison

| Aspect | Fiscal Policy | Monetary Policy |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| **Conducted by** | Government (LegCo, Financial Secretary) | HKMA / central bank |
| **Tools** | Taxation, government spending | Interest rates, money supply |
| **Implementation lag** | Long (budget approval process) | Shorter (operational decisions) |
| **Effectiveness** | Direct impact on AD | Indirect (works through interest rates) |
| **Targets** | Budget balance, specific sectors | Price stability, currency stability |
