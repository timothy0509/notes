# Money and Banking

## Definition and Nature of Money

**Money** is any asset that is widely accepted in payment for goods and services or in repayment of debts.

### Functions of Money

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Medium of exchange** | Eliminates double coincidence of wants (barter problem) |
| **Unit of account** | Standard measure of value; allows comparison of prices |
| **Store of value** | Transfers purchasing power from present to future |
| **Standard of deferred payment** | Facilitates credit transactions and contracts |

> [!info] For an asset to function well as money, it should be: divisible, portable, durable, homogeneous, and scarce.

## Hong Kong Banking System

### Three-Tier System

| Tier | Role | Minimum Capital |
|------|------|-----------------|
| **Licensed Banks** | Full banking services, current/savings accounts | HK\$150M |
| **Restricted Licence Banks** | Wholesale/commercial banking, no current accounts | HK\$100M |
| **Deposit-Taking Companies** | Limited deposit-taking (large deposits, longer maturities) | HK\$25M |

### The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

The HKMA performs **central banking functions** for Hong Kong:

- Maintaining **currency stability** (linked exchange rate)
- Promoting the **safety and stability** of the banking system
- Managing the **Exchange Fund**
- Acting as **lender of last resort**
- Overseeing **payment and settlement systems**

> [!note] Hong Kong has **no formal central bank**. The HKMA, established in 1993, performs these functions alongside the note-issuing banks.

## Money Supply Definitions

| Measure | Components |
|---------|-----------|
| **M1** | Currency held by public + demand deposits (current accounts) |
| **M2** | M1 + savings & time deposits with licensed banks + negotiable certificates of deposit (NCDs) issued by licensed banks |
| **M3** | M2 + deposits with restricted licence banks & DTCs + NCDs issued by these institutions |

> [!tip] M2 is the most commonly referenced measure for Hong Kong. The broadest measure, M3, captures the entire banking system.

## Credit Creation and the Banking Multiplier

### Key Concepts

- **Monetary base (High-powered money)**: Currency in circulation + bank reserves
- **Required reserve ratio (RRR)**: Minimum fraction of deposits banks must hold as reserves
- **Actual reserve ratio**: Fraction actually held (may exceed RRR)

### The Credit Creation Process

When a bank receives a deposit, it can lend out a fraction (excess reserves), which becomes a deposit elsewhere, which can be lent again...

$$\text{Maximum banking multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{Required reserve ratio}}$$

$$\text{Maximum new deposits} = \text{Initial excess reserves} \times \frac{1}{\text{RRR}}$$

> [!example] If RRR = 10% and an initial deposit of \$1,000 is made:
> - Bank lends \$900 (excess reserves)
> - Recipient deposits \$900 in another bank
> - That bank lends \$810
> - Total new deposits created = \$1,000 + \$900 + \$810 + ... = \$1,000 $\times \frac{1}{0.1} = \$10,000$

> [!warning] The **actual** multiplier is usually smaller because of:
> - **Cash leakage** (public holds currency, not deposits)
> - **Excess reserves** (banks hold more than required)
> - **Limited demand for loans**

## Demand for Money

### Motivations for Holding Money (Keynesian)

| Motive | Description | Key Factor |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Transactions demand** | Money needed for everyday purchases | Positively related to **income** ($Y$) |
| **Precautionary demand** | Money held for unexpected expenses | Positively related to **income** |
| **Asset (Speculative) demand** | Money held as an alternative to bonds/other assets | Negatively related to **interest rate** ($i$) |

### Money Demand Function

$$M_d = L(Y, i)$$

where:
- $\frac{\partial M_d}{\partial Y} > 0$ (higher income $\to$ more money demand)
- $\frac{\partial M_d}{\partial i} < 0$ (higher interest rates $\to$ less money demand, as opportunity cost rises)

## Determination of Interest Rate

### The Money Market

Equilibrium interest rate is determined by **money supply** ($M_s$, set by monetary authority) and **money demand** ($M_d$):

$$M_s = M_d$$

> [!info] The **liquidity preference theory** (Keynes): The interest rate is the price of money. If $M_d > M_s$, the interest rate rises until equilibrium is restored.

### Effect of Money Supply Changes

| Change | Effect on $i$ |
|--------|--------------|
| $M_s \uparrow$ (expansionary monetary policy) | $i \downarrow$ |
| $M_s \downarrow$ (contractionary monetary policy) | $i \uparrow$ |

## Hong Kong as a Financial Centre

### Contributing Factors

- **Strategic location** (gateway to China, time zone bridging US and Europe)
- **Rule of law** and independent judiciary
- **Free flow of capital** (no exchange controls)
- **Simple and low tax system**
- **Sound regulatory framework** (HKMA, SFC)
- **Deep and liquid financial markets** (stock exchange, FX, derivatives)
- **Skilled workforce** and international talent pool

### Effects on the HK Economy

> [!tip] Being a financial centre brings substantial benefits:
> - Employment in financial services
> - Tax revenue from the sector
> - Spillover demand for professional services (legal, accounting, consulting)
> - Attracts foreign direct investment

> [!warning] Risks include vulnerability to **global financial shocks** and high **income inequality** (financial sector wages pull away from the rest of the economy).
