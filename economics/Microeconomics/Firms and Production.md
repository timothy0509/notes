# Firms and Production

## Ownership of Firms

### Types of Ownership

| Form | Features | Liability |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | One owner, all profits/losses | **Unlimited** — personal assets at risk |
| **Partnership** | 2+ owners, shared capital/decisions | **Unlimited** (except limited partners) |
| **Limited company** | Shareholders own the firm via shares | **Limited** — loss capped at investment |
| **Public ownership** | Government owns and operates | — |

### Shares vs Bonds as Sources of Capital

| | Shares (Equity) | Bonds (Debt) |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Yes — shareholder is part-owner | No — bondholder is a creditor |
| Return | Dividend (variable, depends on profit) | Interest (fixed) |
| Risk | Higher — residual claimant | Lower — priority over shareholders |
| Voting right | Yes | No |

> [!info] **Limited liability** means shareholders can lose only the value of their shares, not personal assets.

## Types and Stages of Production

| Sector | Activities | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Extraction of natural resources | Farming, fishing, mining |
| **Secondary** | Manufacturing / processing | Car assembly, textile production |
| **Tertiary** | Services | Banking, retail, education, healthcare |

These sectors are **inter-related**: primary provides raw materials → secondary processes them → tertiary distributes and services them. As an economy develops, the share of tertiary output typically rises.

---

## Types of Goods

| Classification | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Producer goods** | Used to produce other goods | Machinery, tools, factory buildings |
| **Consumer goods** | For final consumption | Food, clothes, phones |
| **Private goods** | **Rival** + **Excludable** | Pizza, laptop, haircut |
| **Public goods** | **Non-rival** + **Non-excludable** | National defence, street lighting, radio broadcast |

> [!warning] Public goods lead to the **free-rider problem** — people consume without paying, so the market under-provides them.

## Division of Labour

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Simple** | Each worker performs a single task |
| **Complex** | Tasks are subdivided among specialists |
| **Regional** | Different regions specialise in different products |

### Advantages
- Higher productivity and output
- Better use of comparative advantage
- Time saved switching tasks
- Workers become highly skilled at their task (learning by doing)

### Disadvantages
- Monotony and boredom → lower morale
- Over-dependence on others
- Risk of structural unemployment if demand shifts
- Quality control difficulties

**Limitations**: small market size limits specialisation (the extent of the market limits division of labour).

## Factors of Production

| Factor | Reward | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Labour** | Wage | Supply depends on population, participation rate, hours; **productivity** depends on education, technology, health; **mobility** — occupational (skills) and geographical (willingness to move) |
| **Entrepreneurship** | Profit | Risk-bearing, innovation, decision-making, coordination |
| **Land** | Rent | Fixed supply in the short run; includes all natural resources |
| **Capital** | Interest | **Accumulation** through investment; **depreciation** (wear-and-tear / obsolescence) |

> [!example] Hong Kong features: labour productivity is high due to education; land is extremely scarce → high rent; capital market is well-developed with free capital flow.

### Wage Payment Methods
- **Time rate**: paid by hour/day — encourages quality but not effort
- **Piece rate**: paid per unit output — incentivises quantity but may sacrifice quality

## Short Run vs Long Run

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Short run** | At least **one factor is fixed** (e.g. factory size) |
| **Long run** | **All factors are variable** — the firm can change plant size |

> [!important] "Fixed" does NOT mean unchanging — it means the cost does **not** vary with output.

---

## Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns

In the **short run**, as more of a variable factor is added to a fixed factor, **marginal product** eventually **diminishes**.

```
TP = Total Product
AP = TP / L   (Average Product)
MP = ΔTP / ΔL (Marginal Product)

Stage I:   MP rises → AP rises
Stage II:  MP falls → AP falls, but TP still rising (rational production zone)
Stage III: MP < 0 → TP falls (irrational — too many workers)
```

> [!tip] A profit-maximising firm operates in **Stage II** of production.

## Costs

| Cost | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Fixed cost (FC)** | Does **not** vary with output (e.g. rent) |
| **Variable cost (VC)** | Varies with output (e.g. raw materials) |
| **Total cost (TC)** | FC + VC |
| **Average cost (AC)** | TC / Q |
| **Marginal cost (MC)** | ΔTC / ΔQ — cost of producing one more unit |

> [!note] The MC curve intersects the AC curve at the **minimum** of AC. When MC < AC, AC falls; when MC > AC, AC rises.

## Economies and Diseconomies of Scale

### Internal

| Source | Type |
|---|---|
| **Economies** | Technical (indivisibilities), managerial (specialisation), financial (bulk borrowing), marketing (bulk advertising), risk-bearing (diversification) |
| **Diseconomies** | Coordination/communication difficulties, bureaucratic inefficiency, loss of control |

### External

| Source | Type |
|---|---|
| **Economies** | Infrastructure improvement, concentration of skilled labour, development of supplier networks |
| **Diseconomies** | Congestion, rising factor prices as industry expands |

> [!info] The **long-run average cost (LRAC)** curve slopes downward during economies of scale and upward during diseconomies of scale.

## Expansion and Integration

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Vertical** (backward/forward) | Firm merges with supplier or distributor |
| **Horizontal** | Firm merges with a competitor in the same industry |
| **Lateral** | Firm merges with a firm in a related but different industry |
| **Conglomerate** | Firm merges with a firm in an unrelated industry |

### Motives
- Market power / reduce competition
- Cost savings (synergies)
- Access to new markets or technology
- Risk diversification

## Objectives of Firms

### Profit Maximisation

Under profit maximisation, the firm produces where:

$$ MR = MC $$

> [!example] Given a market price $P$, the firm's marginal revenue $MR = P$ (under perfect competition). It produces where $P = MC$. If the price rises, the profit-maximising quantity rises along the MC curve.

### Other Objectives

- **Sales maximisation** — subject to minimum profit constraint
- **Revenue maximisation** — produce where $MR = 0$
- **Market share growth** — strategic competition
- **Corporate social responsibility (CSR)** — environmental, ethical goals
- **Satisficing** — achieving acceptable rather than maximum outcomes
- **Managerial utility maximisation** — managers pursue their own objectives (salary, perks, status)

> [!important] In the real world, firms often pursue **multiple objectives** simultaneously, constrained by survival and shareholder expectations.
