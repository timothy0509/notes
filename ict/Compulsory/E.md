# Social Implications

> [!abstract] Module Overview
> Compulsory E covers the social, ethical, and legal implications of ICT.
> **Total hours: 8** (3 + 3 + 2)

---

# Technological Innovations

==Technological innovations== refer to new technologies or the novel application of existing technologies that bring about significant changes to society, industries, and daily life.

## AI and Data Science

- ==Artificial Intelligence (AI)== enables machines to mimic human intelligence — learning, reasoning, and self-correction.
- ==Pattern recognition== uses algorithms to identify regularities in data (e.g., facial recognition, speech recognition, medical diagnosis).
- ==Machine learning== is a subset of AI where systems improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.

| Application | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| Healthcare | AI-assisted diagnosis, drug discovery |
| Finance | Fraud detection, algorithmic trading |
| Transport | Self-driving cars, route optimisation |
| Education | Personalised learning, automated marking |

## 3D Printing

- ==Additive manufacturing== builds objects layer by layer from a digital model.
- Materials: plastics, metals, concrete, biological tissue.
- Applications: prototyping, custom prosthetics, architectural models, food printing.

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Know at least 2–3 real-world applications of 3D printing. The keyword ==layer-by-layer== often earns marks.

## Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR)

| Technology | Definition | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ==AR== | Overlays digital content onto the real world | Pokémon GO, Google Maps Live View |
| ==VR== | Fully immersive digital environment | Meta Quest, flight simulators |
| ==MR== (Mixed Reality) | Combines real and virtual worlds | Microsoft HoloLens |

> [!info] Practical Experience
> Students should have hands-on experience with at least one of these technologies, e.g., using AR apps on a phone or a VR headset in a lab.

# Health and Ergonomics

## Health Hazards of ICT Use

| Hazard | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| ==Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)== | Pain from repeated motions (typing, mouse use) |
| ==Computer Vision Syndrome== | Eye strain from prolonged screen time |
| ==Back and neck pain== | Poor posture from hunching over devices |
| ==Sleep disruption== | Blue light from screens affects melatonin |
| ==Obesity== | Sedentary lifestyle from excessive screen time |
| ==Social isolation== | Over-reliance on virtual interactions |

## Ergonomics: Workstation Setup

==Ergonomics== is the science of designing the workstation to fit the user, reducing discomfort and injury.

> [!tip] Ideal Workstation Checklist
> - **Chair**: Feet flat on floor, thighs parallel to ground, lumbar support
> - **Monitor**: Arm's length away, top of screen at or slightly below eye level
> - **Keyboard**: Elbows at 90°, wrists straight, keyboard at elbow height
> - **Mouse**: Close to keyboard, at same height, within easy reach
> - **Lighting**: Avoid glare on screen, use indirect lighting
> - **Breaks**: Follow the ==20-20-20 rule== — every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds

### Reducing RSI

| Good Practice | Bad Practice |
| :--- | :--- |
| Use an ergonomic keyboard/mouse | Flat laptop keyboard for hours |
| Take regular breaks | Continuous typing without rest |
| Alternate tasks | Repetitive same motion all day |
| Use voice input occasionally | Typing with bent wrists |

> [!warning] Exam Tip
> Ergonomics questions often ask you to ==propose good practices== or ==identify hazards== from a diagram of a workstation. Know the ideal setup by heart.

## Software User-Friendliness

Good software design contributes to health:
- ==Intuitive interfaces== reduce cognitive load and frustration.
- ==Keyboard shortcuts== reduce mouse dependence.
- ==Adjustable font sizes== and ==dark mode== reduce eye strain.
- ==Customisable layouts== accommodate different needs.

# Equity and Access

## The Digital Divide

The ==digital divide== is the gap between those who have access to ICT and those who do not, including:

| Dimension | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Physical access** | Availability of hardware and internet connectivity |
| **Skills access** | Digital literacy and ICT competency |
| **Usage access** | Quality and purpose of ICT use |

### Factors Contributing to the Digital Divide

- ==Socioeconomic status==: Lower-income households cannot afford devices or broadband.
- ==Geography==: Rural areas may lack infrastructure.
- ==Age==: Elderly populations may lack digital skills.
- ==Education==: Less educated individuals may struggle with ICT use.

## Gender Equity

- Historical gender gap in ICT participation is narrowing.
- Stereotypes and cultural factors still discourage some groups from pursuing ICT careers.
- Initiatives: coding camps for girls, scholarships, mentorship programmes.

## Access for People with Disabilities

| Need | Assistive Technology |
| :--- | :--- |
| Visual impairment | Screen readers (JAWS, NVDA), Braille displays |
| Hearing impairment | Captioning, visual alerts, sign language apps |
| Motor impairment | Voice recognition, eye-tracking, switch devices |
| Cognitive impairment | Simplified interfaces, text-to-speech |

> [!info] Hong Kong Context
> Hong Kong's ==Disability Discrimination Ordinance== (Cap. 487) requires equal access to services, including digital ones. The ==Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)== provide international standards.

## Global Perspectives

- Developing nations benefit from ICT for education, healthcare, and economic growth.
- ==One Laptop per Child (OLPC)== and similar initiatives aim to bridge the gap.
- Mobile technology has leapfrogged traditional infrastructure in many regions.

# Ethical Issues

## Responsible Use of ICT

Ethical behaviour in ICT includes:
- Respecting ==intellectual property== (see below).
- Protecting ==personal privacy==.
- Using ICT for ==legitimate purposes== only.
- Being ==honest and transparent== in digital communications.

## Privacy

- ==Personal data== must be collected, stored, and used responsibly.
- See [[Compulsory/A]] for data validation — accuracy relates to ethical data handling.
- Hong Kong's ==Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance== (Cap. 486) governs data collection and use.

> [!warning] Key Regulation
> The ==Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO)== requires:
> 1. Purpose and manner of collection must be lawful
> 2. Data must be used only for the intended purpose
> 3. Data must be accurate and kept up to date
> 4. Data must not be kept longer than necessary
> 5. Data must be protected against unauthorised access

## Cyberbullying

- ==Cyberbullying== is the use of ICT to harass, threaten, or humiliate others.
- Platforms: social media, messaging apps, online forums.
- Consequences: psychological harm, legal action under the ==Crimes Ordinance== (Cap. 212).

> [!example] Real-World Cases
> In Hong Kong, several high-profile cyberbullying cases have led to criminal charges. Students should be aware that online actions have real-world consequences.

## Intellectual Property in Practice

- Downloading copyrighted material without permission is ==piracy==.
- Using someone else's work without attribution is ==plagiarism==.
- Sharing copyrighted content on social media may constitute ==infringement==.

See [[Compulsory/C#Network Security]] for related security and ethical considerations.

# Intellectual Property

## What is Intellectual Property?

==Intellectual Property (IP)== refers to creations of the mind — inventions, literary works, designs, symbols, and names used in commerce.

| Type | What it Protects | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ==Copyright== | Original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works | Books, music, software, films |
| ==Trademark== | Brand names, logos, slogans | Apple logo, Nike swoosh |
| ==Patent== | Inventions and processes | New drug formulas, technologies |
| ==Trade Secret== | Confidential business information | Coca-Cola recipe |

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> For HKDSE, focus primarily on ==copyright== and ==software licensing==. Trademarks and patents are less frequently tested but good to know.

## Software Licensing

| Licence Type | Description | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ==Freeware== | Free to use, no modification allowed | Free |
| ==Shareware== | Free trial, then paid | Paid after trial |
| ==Open Source== | Free to use, modify, and redistribute | Free |
| ==Proprietary/Copyrighted== | Paid licence, no modification allowed | Paid |

> [!warning] Common Exam Trap
> ==Freeware≠ Free Software==. Freeware may be free to use but not free to modify. ==Open source== allows modification and redistribution. Know the distinctions.

## Copyright Infringement and Piracy

==Copyright infringement== occurs when copyrighted material is used without permission. ==Piracy== is the illegal copying, distribution, or use of copyrighted software/media.

### Examples of Infringement

- Copying software from a friend's computer without a licence.
- Downloading movies/music from illegal streaming sites.
- Using copyrighted images in a project without a licence.
- Sharing e-books or PDFs online without authorisation.

### Ways to Reduce IP Theft

| Method | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| ==Digital watermarking== | Invisible/visible marks embedded in files to identify ownership |
| ==Digital signature== | Cryptographic verification of authenticity and integrity |
| ==DRM (Digital Rights Management)== | Technology that controls access to copyrighted content |
| ==Licence agreements== | Legal terms specifying how software can be used |
| ==Patent/Copyright registration== | Legal protection enabling enforcement |

## Legal Consequences in Hong Kong

> [!danger] Copyright Ordinance (Cap. 528)
> Under Hong Kong law, copyright infringement can result in:
> - **Civil liability**: Damages, injunctions, account of profits
> - **Criminal prosecution**: Up to ==4 years imprisonment== and a fine of up to ==HK$50,000== per infringing copy for first offence
> - For commercial-scale infringement: Up to ==HK$500,000 fine== and ==8 years imprisonment==
>
> Ignorance of the law is ==not== a valid defence.
